# Vorago: Content Plan (Draft)

> Psychology, persuasion and negotiation. Draft plan for Canto I, plus ideas for later Cantos. Last updated: 2026-09-29

**Format:** 1 Canto = 15 days × 7 notes = **105 notes**. 2 Cantos a month.

---

## Structure

- Every day has **7 notes: 3 Psychology · 2 Persuasion · 2 Negotiation**, always in that order (psychology leads; the other two build on it). Over 15 days: **45 / 30 / 30**
- Topics on the same day are picked to **talk to each other** where possible (e.g. loss aversion in psychology on the day of BATNA and walk-away points)
- Every note carries an **evidence tag**:
  - **Solid**: holds up well; replicated, or a well-established idea/technique
  - **Debated**: real research behind it, but the size, meaning or reliability is argued over
  - **Legend**: a famous story or claim that doesn't hold up the way it's usually told (the note explains what's actually true)
- **Tone:** "see the hidden moves so they don't work on you", not a manipulation manual
- **Overlap with Retia:** Retia's Folio I already covers anchoring, confirmation bias, motivated reasoning, availability, Dunning–Kruger and sunk costs, so Canto I skips them (anchoring comes back from the negotiation side as *First offers*). Retia's round 7 (next summer) plans Cialdini and ethos / pathos / logos, which Vorago now covers: worth swapping in Retia later

## How to write a note (same as Retia, plus the tag)

- **In short:** one sentence, the core idea
- **Explained:** 2–4 sentences: how it works, who found it, the nuance or common confusion. For Debated / Legend notes, this is where the honest version goes
- **Example:** a concrete everyday or real-world case
- **Why it matters:** where you'll meet it, or how to spot / use / resist it
- Length about **110–150 words**; each page (In short + Explained / Example + Why it matters) about **75 words** so nothing scrolls
- Title = the thing itself, and **unique** (progress and Recall use the title: "What is… loss aversion?")

Template:
```
### Psychology | Loss aversion
**Evidence:** Debated
**In short:** …
**Explained:** …
**Example:** …
**Why it matters:** …
```

### Notes to self for writing (the honest versions)

- **Loss aversion:** real, but how big and how universal is argued over; not "losses always hurt twice as much"
- **Stanford Prison Experiment:** guards were coached toward cruelty and some behaviour was acted; it never went through normal peer review as a study of "natural" cruelty
- **Power posing:** the hormone results failed to replicate and a co-author disowned them; a small "feel more powerful" effect may remain
- **Bystander effect:** the lab effect is real, but the Kitty Genovese story that made it famous was badly exaggerated, and CCTV studies show people usually do help
- **Milgram:** obedience was real, but many participants doubted it was real pain, and results varied a lot between versions
- **Ego depletion / Marshmallow test:** large replications found the willpower effect near zero; the marshmallow test's life predictions shrink a lot once family background is counted
- **Behavioural priming:** "reading old-age words makes you walk slower" failed to replicate; ordinary word priming is fine
- **Hawthorne effect:** the original factory data don't show the famous effect, though being watched can change behaviour
- **Subliminal advertising:** James Vicary's 1957 "eat popcorn" study was made up, and he later admitted it
- **7-38-55 rule:** Mehrabian's numbers only applied to people judging feelings from single words said in a mismatched tone, not to all communication
- **The "because" effect:** one small 1978 photocopier study (Langer); charming, but thin evidence
- **Tactical empathy / labelling emotions / the flinch / good cop:** mostly practitioner advice (FBI negotiator Chris Voss and sales lore), with limited research; say so
- **First offers:** research says making the first offer usually helps *if you know the value*; if you don't, let them go first
