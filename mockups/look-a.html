Anchoring
In short

The first number you hear pulls your judgment toward it, even when that number is meaningless.

Explained

In a 1974 study, Tversky and Kahneman spun a wheel rigged to stop at 10 or 65, then asked people what share of African countries were in the UN. Those who saw 10 guessed about 25%; those who saw 65 guessed about 45%. The brain starts from whatever number is on the table and adjusts, but not far enough.

Day I0 / 7
PsychologySolid
Anchoring
Example

A jacket tagged “Was $200, now $120” feels like a bargain, even if it was never worth more than $80. The $200 is the anchor. Car prices, salaries and house sales all start the same way: the first number sets the range.

Why it matters

In a negotiation, if you know what something is worth, name the first number and make it bold but defensible. If they anchor first, don't haggle from their number: restate your own.
